Spatial Analysis & Predictive Modeling of Internal Migration in the Czech Republic (1995–2019)

Masaryk University — Faculty of Economics and Administration | Defended / Finalized (2024–2026)

25 Years
Administrative Microdata (1995–2019)
PPML
Pseudo-Maximum Likelihood + Fixed Effects
2015–2019
Ex-Post Validation Window

📦 Data & Granularity

  • Source: Comprehensive administrative microdata covering individual moves across all municipalities in the Czech Republic.
  • Decomposition: Disaggregated the general population into distinct age cohorts to analyze generational migration sensitivity.
  • Spatial Resistance: Modeled physical distance, administrative borders (districts/regions), and local proximity as spatial friction factors.

⚙️ Methodology & Modeling

  • Structural Gravity Model: Formulated empirical models to capture bilateral flow dynamics between origin and destination nodes.
  • PPML Estimation: Employed Poisson Pseudo-Maximum Likelihood with time-specific fixed effects in R to handle zero flows and heteroskedasticity.
  • Bias Reduction: Eliminated aggregation bias by validating micro-level flows against traditional macro-level models.

📈 Key Findings & Outcomes

  • Universal Friction vs. Boundary Elasticity: Physical distance serves as a universal barrier across all cohorts; however, senior cohorts exhibit significantly higher attachment to their home district boundaries.
  • Predictive Accuracy: Ex-post predictive validation (2015–2019) confirmed that cohort decomposition significantly enhances the capture of high-volume flows.
  • Structural Insights: Confirmed that while historical relationships reliably predict stable spatial flows, they encounter specific limits during abrupt socio-economic shifts.

📦 Data & Scope

  • Scope: Multidimensional socio-demographic, economic, and regional indicator datasets for the Czech Republic.
  • Feature Engineering: Data normalization, z-score standardization, outlier detection, and correlation matrix analysis.

⚙️ Algorithms & Evaluation

  • Partitioning & Hierarchical: Implemented and compared k-means, medoid partitioning, and agglomerative hierarchical clustering (Ward’s method).
  • Validation Metrics: Utilized Elbow method, Silhouette coefficients, and Cophenetic correlation to determine optimal cluster counts ($k$).

📈 Key Outcomes

  • Identified discrete regional clusters reflecting socio-economic disparities and spatial cohesion without prior class labels.
  • Delivered a reproducible R script framework for automated clustering and spatial visualization of municipal data.
